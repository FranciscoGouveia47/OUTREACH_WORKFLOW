Contact-fit and personalisation guardrails:
- Before drafting, perform a local relevance check using only the row context.
- Do not assume the contact is suitable merely because their company, title,
  industry label, or department sounds adjacent to the product.
- The email may only make claims directly supported by the individual's own
  role, responsibility, public profile, company page, or recorded evidence note.
- If the row does not contain direct individual or role-based evidence that the
  recipient is a plausible buyer, partner, or referrer, do not produce a ready
  email. Set AI Ready For Human Review to No and add AI Risk Flags such as weak
  individual evidence, organisation-level evidence, wrong-person risk,
  overbroad personalisation, or routing-only contact.
- Do not say the recipient owns a budget, leads procurement, evaluates vendors,
  manages a problem area, or needs the offer unless the row evidence supports
  that claim.
- If the person appears to be an administrative contact, general mailbox, or
  routing point, draft only a referral request or mark the row not ready.
- Never create personalisation by concatenating workbook fields, for example
  "Given your role as [Role], together with your work in [Sector]...".
- Do not describe the evidence source to the recipient. Avoid phrases such as
  "the workbook says", "the row lists", "your LinkedIn says", or "the source
  describes".
- Avoid generic fit formulas such as "your work aligns with our solution" when
  no specific problem, responsibility, or business context is supported.
- Prefer one narrow, defensible reason for outreach over a list of broad claims.
- Keep the tone useful and commercial, not exaggerated. Do not imply an existing
  relationship, prior conversation, endorsement, urgency, scarcity, or consent
  that has not happened.
- For organisation or shared-inbox recipients, use collective or routing
  language and do not imply the mailbox owner personally has the need.
